Problems worth solving

Each problem comes from a learning health network working to improve outcomes for patients and families. The person named on it would welcome help from someone with informatics expertise.

  1. Choose a problem that fits what you know.
  2. Write to the person named on it. The button starts the email for you.
  3. Tell them you came from AMIA.

All problems

    On the slide

    1. Community health

      How can families get “Amazon-like” access to services such as home visiting, Head Start, and public benefits?

      Rob Kahn · Cincinnati Children’s · All Children Thrive

    2. Pediatric lupus

      Quality measures need a disease activity score the EHR doesn’t capture. Can ambient listening and AI fill it in?

      Emily Smitherman · University of Alabama at Birmingham · PR-COIN

      The SLEDAI disease activity score depends on data not typically recorded in the EHR.

    3. Kidney transplant

      Can behavioral, biomarker, and clinical data predict a rejection a month before it happens?

      David Hooper · Primary Children’s Hospital, Salt Lake City · IROC

    4. Youth mental health

      How can a hospital join several networks without months of negotiation for each?

      Leslie Hulvershorn · Indiana University · Youth Mental Health Action Network

      The Youth Mental Health Action Network is launching multiple networks at once: substance use, disruptive early childhood behavior, suicide prevention, and integrating behavioral health care into primary care.

    More from the networks

    1. Pediatric liver transplant

      Could a specialized AI assistant support pediatric transplant recipients’ transition to independent, sustainable management of their own health care in young adulthood?

      Emily Perito · UCSF Benioff Children’s Hospital · Starzl Network

      Also from this network

      • Can real-time decision support for organ acceptance combine the patient’s illness trajectory and current status with data about the organ offer?

    2. Youth suicide prevention

      How can we build a system that can follow every patient at risk for suicide from first identification through personalized care, and measure the outcomes, as we do for other life-threatening conditions?

      Lisa Horowitz · Children’s Hospital Colorado · Suicide Prevention Improvement Network (SPIN)

    3. Rare disease

      Can standardized outcome definitions, including patient-reported outcomes, show disease progression and treatment response?

      Jim Squires · UPMC Children’s Hospital of Pittsburgh · Autoimmune hepatitis network (A-LiNK)

    4. Bipolar disorder

      How can a clinician learn, before the visit, that the patient recently had lab work done by another provider?

      Andrew Nierenberg · Massachusetts General Hospital · Bipolar Action Network

      Knowing about recent outside results before the visit would make the visit more effective.

    5. Food allergy

      How might we design an automated system that identifies infants and young toddlers within our network who appear to be at high risk for food allergy and pushes prevention recommendations to the PCP (or directly to the caregiver)?

      Wayne Shreffler · Mass General Brigham · SAFARI Network

    6. Central line infections

      How can we reliably identify, in real time, which patients with central lines are entering a period of substantially increased CLABSI risk, so that teams can target additional preventive resources when and where they are most needed?

      Anne Lyren · Solutions for Patient Safety

    If none of these fit

    These are the problems as the networks see them today. Once you work inside a network, you may see a better one. If you do, tell the people working on it.
